# Content Plan

Page-by-page application of the USP. Read `BRAND-VOICE.md` and `CASE-STUDIES.md` first.

**The test for every page:** what does the other side of *this* table know that our reader
doesn't? A page that can't answer that shouldn't ship.

---

## `/` — Home

**Hero:** her line, verbatim or near it.

> Most people think negotiating is just about getting the price down.
> It's not.

Sub: the askable-things framing. Primary CTA is the phone number (Follow Up Boss tracking
number). Secondary CTA is the lead magnet.

**Immediately below:** the three case studies, plainly presented. Lead with **Case 2**
(roof/HVAC/warranty/refrigerator) — most surprising, not biggest. Then Case 1, then Case 3.
Results disclaimer adjacent.

**Then:** four pillar cards. Then a short trust strip — 98.84% list-to-sale, 73+
transactions, 105 five-star reviews. Then two testimonials. Then contact.

Do not put a stat wall above the fold. The story earns the stats; the stats don't earn
attention on their own.

---

## `/negotiation` — the lead magnet landing page

**The highest-converting asset on the site. Build this early.**

**Offer:** *"The 19 Things Besides Price You Can Negotiate"*

Her caption's promise turned into something downloadable. It is genuinely useful, it
*demonstrates* the USP instead of asserting it, and it is evergreen — the list barely
changes decade to decade.

The number in the title should match the actual count in the guide. If the real list is 14
or 23, retitle it. Never inflate to hit a rounder number.

**Content:** grouped by category — condition and repairs, closing costs and concessions,
timeline and possession, inclusions, contingencies, new-construction-specific incentives.
Each item one or two sentences: what it is, when it's available, why sellers say yes.

**Page structure:** the caption reframe → what's in the guide → Case 2 as proof it works →
form → results disclaimer.

**Form:** name, email, phone, buyer/seller, TCPA consent. → FUB as lead type `guide`.

## `/new-construction` — PILLAR

*The table:* the builder's sales office negotiates every day of the week. The buyer has
never negotiated with one.

Anchor: **Case 3** ($50K incentives, 3% closing costs, refrigerator).

Cover: why the on-site agent is not your agent; what builder incentives actually are and
why they're rarely volunteered; lender tie-ins; upgrade pricing; why walking in
unrepresented usually costs money. Name the ten builders — that list is unmatched locally.

> The builder's rep has negotiated a hundred of these this year. You've negotiated none.
> I've done 17.

## `/relocation` — PILLAR

*The table:* everyone in the transaction is local except the client.

Out-of-state buyer orientation: Charlotte submarket geography, the NC/SC decision, commute
realities, timeline for a remote purchase, what she does on the ground when the client
can't be there.

Pair with the **Kathy Hannan testimonial** (Phoenix, Hurricane Ian showing) — direct
evidence for this exact claim.

Downloadable relocation guide → FUB lead type `relocation`.

---

## `/carolinas-border` — PILLAR

*The table:* two states, two tax regimes, two rulebooks.

NC vs SC on property tax treatment, vehicle registration, income tax, school district
structure, closing process differences. Fort Mill, Tega Cay, Indian Land, Lake Wylie,
Waxhaw. 12 closings in the Fort Mill corridor.

Dual licensure is a genuine moat — most Charlotte agents can only work one side.

**Fair housing caution:** discuss tax, commute, cost, and housing stock. Never school
"quality," neighborhood "safety," or community demographics.

## `/areas/[slug]`

One per market in `CLAUDE.md` §5. Each must be genuinely distinct — housing stock, price
bands, commute, what typically trades there, which negotiation levers tend to exist in that
submarket. That last angle is the USP applied locally and is what will separate these from
the thousands of templated Charlotte neighborhood pages.

Thin duplicated pages hurt more than they help. If there isn't real content for a market,
don't publish the page.

## `/contact`

Phone first, large. Form secondary. Brokerage name, address, both license numbers, Equal
Housing logo.

Still true, with one change: the form is no longer only here. `docs/CONTACT-STRATEGY.md`
puts the intake at the foot of every page that does the persuading, because a reader who
has just finished a pillar page should not have to navigate anywhere to act. This page is
the destination for nav traffic, not the only door.
