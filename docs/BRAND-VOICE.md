# Brand & Voice — Jasmine Garcia

Read alongside `CLAUDE.md`. This document governs every word on the site.

---

## 1. The USP

> **The other side of the table does this for a living.
> They know what's askable. Now you do too.**

### Where it came from

Her own Instagram caption, which is the origin of this entire direction:

> Most people think negotiating is just about getting the price down. It's not.
>
> Sometimes the biggest savings come from things buyers don't even know to ask for.
>
> Every house is different. Every seller is different. That's why every negotiation
> should be different.

### Why this and not something else

**"Great negotiator" is not a USP.** Every agent in Charlotte claims it. A claim anyone
can make is table stakes, not differentiation.

**This names the client's actual fear.** Not "I want a nice house." The real one, the one
they don't say out loud: *I don't know what I don't know, and the people across the table
do this every day.* Copy that names an unspoken fear converts. Copy that lists awards does not.

**It claims parity, not superiority.** She is not saying she is better than the client.
She is saying she is the counterweight to the professional across from them. Service
posture, not flex. This is what keeps it from reading as arrogant.

**It is evergreen.** Which levers matter shifts with the market — concessions when rates
are high, builder incentives when inventory sits, repairs when inspection leverage is
strong. That a once-in-a-lifetime buyer never knows the full list of levers does not shift.
This positioning was true in 1986 and will be true in 2046.

**It generates a specific question in the reader's head:** *what am I not asking for?*
That question is what makes someone fill out a form. "Are you a good negotiator?" is not.

### The test for every page

> What does the other side of *this* table know that our reader doesn't?

If a page can't answer that, it isn't doing its job.

## 2. Voice rules

**Her own writing is the model.** From the caption above: short declarative sentences.
A reframe, then proof. Parallel structure. No adjectives doing work that numbers should do.

### Do
- Short sentences. Fragments are fine.
- Reframe, then evidence. ("Most people think X. It's not.")
- Concrete nouns and real numbers
- Second person. Talk to one reader, not an audience.
- Warm and direct. She is a mother of twins and an HOA board president — competent and
  human, not luxury-aloof.
- Let silence do work. The strongest proof points need no adjective attached.

### Don't
- Stacked superlatives
- Exclamation points
- Rhetorical questions as headers ("Ready to find your dream home?")
- Any sentence that would fit unchanged on a competitor's site

### Banned words and phrases
`nestled` · `boasts` · `dream home` · `passionate about helping` ·
`your trusted Charlotte REALTOR®` · `unparalleled` · `luxury lifestyle` ·
`let me help you find` · `whether you're buying or selling` · `hidden gem` ·
`I traded one classroom for another` · `real estate found me` · `negotiation queen`

### Never claim
Credentials, awards, designations, or statistics not documented in `CLAUDE.md` §5 or
`CASE-STUDIES.md`. If copy needs a number you don't have, write `TODO(verify)`.

## 3. The origin story — handle with care

**RESOLVED 2026-08-07. Version A approved, with one adjustment: the copy must carry her
tenacity, not read as quitting.** The burnout stays — it is the honest answer and the whole
point — but the page states plainly that ten years is a long time to do that job for that
money and that she did not leave early. What she left was the job. She did not leave the
work.

Shipped in `app/about/page.tsx`.

The rule against inventing a third version still stands. This is a framing adjustment to
Version A, not a substitution of a nicer reason.

**Superseded:** her live Placester bio read *"Jasmine Garcia didn't leave teaching — she
simply brought her classroom skills into the world of real estate."* That is the collapsed
version this section warns against, and it contradicts the documented facts. It retires with
that site.

### The facts
She taught special education for ten years. She left because of **burnout, low pay, and
stress.** That is the real reason.

### Why this matters to the copy
An earlier draft framed her exit as a search for a new room to advocate in. That is not
true, and a founding story that isn't true is a liability — she would have to perform it
at every listing appointment, and performed stories read as performed.

**Keep two facts separate.** Why she left (burnout) and what she brought with her (the
skill) are different things. Only the second is the USP. Collapsing them produces the
sanded-down version every ex-teacher agent in America writes.

### Version A — candid (preferred, pending her approval)

> I taught special education for ten years. I left because I was burned out and
> underpaid — that's the honest answer, and I'll give it to anyone who asks.
>
> What I didn't leave behind was what those ten years taught me. I sat in a lot of
> rooms where one parent, frightened and outnumbered, faced a table of people who did
> this every week and spoke a language they didn't. My job was to make sure the person
> with the most at stake wasn't the least prepared.
>
> I do the same thing now. The room just changed.

**Why this is strong:** the candor is itself the trust signal. An agent who tells you the
unflattering version of her own story is an agent who will tell you the unflattering
version of the inspection report. That is precisely what a buyer is scanning for.

There is also a second proof point buried in it — she left a career because it didn't pay,
then became a top-five producer in a 2,500-family brokerage. **Do not state this.** Let the
reader do the math. Saying it out loud kills it.

### Version B — brief (use if she is not comfortable with Version A)

> I taught special education for ten years before real estate. Ten years of sitting in
> rooms where one person, frightened and outnumbered, faced a table of professionals who
> did this every week. My job was to make sure the person with the most at stake wasn't
> the least prepared.
>
> The room changed. The job didn't.

Shorter, still true, still works. **We do not invent a third version.** If Version A is
too exposed for her, we say less — we do not substitute a nicer reason.

### The IEP parallel
The structural analogy is the engine of the whole brand, and no competitor can copy it:

> One party has everything at stake and has done this once.
> The other party does this every week and knows the vocabulary.

IEP meeting. Builder's sales office. Listing negotiation. Same asymmetry, different room.

## 4. Supporting story elements

**HOA board presidency** — four years, 105-unit Ayrsley townhome community. Quiet
supporting proof, not a headline. It says she can hold a room and mediate among neighbors
who must keep living next to each other. About page only.

**Mastermind book club** — founded 2018, still leads it. Humanizing detail. About page only.

**Twin daughters, homeowner in Charlotte, five years in the market, dual NC/SC licensure** —
use where relevant, never as a stat block.

**Where she lives — Ayrsley, in Steele Creek, since June 2021.** Reported by Bill
2026-08-19, and recorded here so §6 permits its use. Ayrsley is a mixed-use development in
southwest Charlotte off I-485 and South Tryon — five apartment and townhome communities
alongside restaurants, offices, hotels, and Ayrsley Grand Cinemas 14, close to Charlotte
Douglas. The HOA presidency above is one of those communities.

This is the strongest thing available for `/areas/steele-creek`, because residency is
first-person knowledge of the commute and the housing stock rather than a data pull.
`docs/AREAS-SPEC.md` §10 has the application.

**Two limits.** Say Ayrsley and say Steele Creek; do **not** name which of the five
communities she presided over, and do not run the "105-unit" count next to the residency
claim on a public page. Named community plus unit count plus tenure narrows to a household,
and this is a woman who meets strangers at vacant properties for a living. The About page
keeps the presidency as it is; the area page states residency and stops.

Not a §7 rule — no advertising regulation is at stake. A safety default, and hers to
overrule if she wants to.
